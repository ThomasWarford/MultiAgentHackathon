## Summary

The research paper by Kuryla et al. investigates the accuracy of density functional theory (DFT) forces in molecular datasets used for training machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs). The study highlights significant discrepancies in DFT force components across several datasets, including SPICE, Transition1x, ANI-1x, AIMNet2, and others. These discrepancies are evidenced by nonzero net forces, which indicate numerical errors in DFT calculations. The authors recompute forces using more reliable DFT settings and find substantial errors, with discrepancies ranging from 1.7 meV/Å in the SPICE dataset to 33.2 meV/Å in the ANI-1x dataset. This finding underscores the necessity for well-converged DFT data to ensure the accuracy of MLIPs, as errors in DFT forces can significantly affect the quality of the trained models.

The study further explores the sources of these errors, identifying issues such as the use of the RIJCOSX approximation and inadequate DFT grid settings. By adjusting these computational settings, the authors demonstrate that it is possible to reduce net forces and improve the accuracy of DFT force calculations. The paper provides guidelines for improving the quality of DFT data in existing and future datasets, emphasizing the importance of high-quality DFT settings to minimize numerical errors. This research is particularly relevant for the development of MLIPs, as it highlights the impact of DFT force inaccuracies on the performance of machine learning models in molecular simulations.

The authors present an alchemical free energy protocol that leverages a pretrained, transferable MLP model, specifically the MACE-OFF24-SC, which incorporates scalable soft-core interactions. This approach allows for the rigorous calculation of free energy differences in condensed phase systems, achieving subchemical accuracy for a diverse range of organic molecules.

The study demonstrates the efficacy of the MACE-OFF24-SC model in predicting solvation free energies in both aqueous and nonaqueous solvents, outperforming classical force fields like OpenFF 2.1 and GAFF2. The model's ability to accurately predict hydration free energies and distribution coefficients (log P) for drug-like molecules highlights its potential for applications in drug discovery. The research underscores the advantages of MLPs in capturing complex intermolecular interactions directly from quantum mechanical data, offering a promising alternative to empirical force fields for computational chemistry and pharmaceutical research.

### limitations-of-cluster-trained-mlips-for-liquid-density-and-diffusivity

## Summary

This study investigates the limitations of machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) trained on cluster data for predicting liquid properties such as density and diffusivity. The research focuses on ethylene carbonate (EC) and ethyl methyl carbonate (EMC), which are relevant for battery applications. The authors trained MLIPs using the MACE architecture on two types of data sets: periodic structures and molecular clusters. They assessed the uncertainties in the MLIP models and their impact on molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of liquid properties. The study found that while MLIPs trained on periodic data provided stable and reliable predictions, those trained on cluster data exhibited significant uncertainties, particularly in diffusivity and density predictions. This sensitivity was attributed to the random training seed and data selection, highlighting the challenges of using cluster data for bulk liquid simulations.

The research underscores the importance of selecting appropriate training data and electronic structure methods for MLIP development. The study demonstrates that MLIPs trained on periodic data can effectively compare different density functional theory (DFT) functionals, offering a pathway for benchmarking DFT methods against experimental observations. However, the cluster-trained MLIPs showed large variations in predicted properties, suggesting that additional data augmentation or methodological adjustments are necessary for reliable bulk liquid simulations. This work is particularly relevant for the development of MLIPs in condensed matter simulations and emphasizes the need for careful consideration of training data representation and long-range interaction effects.

### towards-routine-condensed-phase-simulations-with-delta-learned-coupled-cluster-accuracy-application-to-liquid-water

## Summary

The research paper presents a novel approach to simulate liquid water with high accuracy using machine learning potentials (MLPs) that achieve coupled cluster theory with single, double, and perturbative triple excitations [CCSD(T)] accuracy. This method addresses the computational challenges associated with applying CCSD(T) to condensed phase systems by integrating machine learning with local correlation approximations. The authors demonstrate that their approach can accurately predict structural and transport properties of liquid water, including its density maximum, in close agreement with experimental data. This is achieved by combining machine learning potentials with nuclear quantum effects, enabling constant-pressure simulations that are crucial for predicting isothermal isobaric bulk properties.

The significance of this work lies in its potential to make high-accuracy simulations of condensed phase systems more routine and computationally feasible. By leveraging a Δ-learning strategy, the authors efficiently train MLPs to reproduce CCSD(T) level accuracy using a baseline model trained on density functional theory (DFT) data. This approach reduces the computational cost significantly, making it possible to perform large-scale molecular dynamics simulations with CCSD(T) accuracy. The study provides a practical blueprint for developing CCSD(T)-based MLPs, which could be extended to other complex systems, thereby advancing the field of computational chemistry and materials science.
